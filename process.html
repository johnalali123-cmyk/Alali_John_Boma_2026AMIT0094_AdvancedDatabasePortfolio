solas. The afterlife of a shoe

End of life · A better way back

Worn out?
Back to earth.

Good things come full circle. When your pair has walked its last mile, four small steps help its natural materials return to the soil.

01
First things first

Wear them out properly

Wait until the sole is flat, the upper is worn, and there are no more miles left in them. A wearable shoe still has life to give.

02
A little prep

Take off what comes off

Remove the laces and lift out the insole. They break down at a different pace, so set them aside to compost separately.

03
Smaller is faster

Cut the pair down

Snip the upper into strips and the sole into palm-sized pieces. Smaller pieces break down sooner and mix more easily through the heap.

04
Let nature work

Into the compost they go

Add the pieces to a home compost heap or green-waste collection, cover them, and keep the pile damp. Turn it now and then.

A note on timing In a working compost heap, natural materials break down over months rather than decades. The sugarcane sole goes first; the eucalyptus upper follows.

A softer landing

Good shoes.

Give the natural parts the right conditions: air, a little moisture, and time. A compost heap does the rest.
